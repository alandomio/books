# Dossier Capitolo 5 — Dall'Addestramento alla Conversazione

### [PUBLIC]

**Source:** Ouyang et al., "Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback", arXiv:2203.02155 (March 4, 2022; published in Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 35 / NeurIPS 2022). | **Fact:** InstructGPT was created by fine-tuning GPT-3 using supervised learning on demonstrations and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to align model outputs with user intent.
**Source:** Ouyang et al. (2022), arXiv:2203.02155, Figure 1. | **Fact:** When prompted with "Explain the moon landing to a 6 year old in a few sentences", the base GPT-3 (175B) model did not answer the prompt but continued generating parallel prompts ("Explain the theory of gravity to a 6 year old. Explain the theory of relativity to a 6 year old in a few sentences. Explain the big bang theory to a 6 year old. Explain evolution to a 6 year old."). InstructGPT (175B) answered directly: "People went to the moon, and they took pictures of what they saw, and sent them back to the earth so we could all see them."
**Source:** Ouyang et al. (2022), arXiv:2203.02155, Section 1 & Section 4.1. | **Fact:** In human evaluations conducted on the distribution of prompts submitted to the OpenAI API, outputs from a 1.3B parameter InstructGPT model were preferred by labelers over outputs from the 175B parameter base GPT-3 model, despite having more than 100 times fewer parameters.
**Source:** Ouyang et al. (2022), arXiv:2203.02155, Abstract & Section 4.1. | **Fact:** Outputs from the 175B InstructGPT model were preferred to outputs from the 175B GPT-3 baseline 85 ± 3% of the time, and preferred 71 ± 4% of the time compared to few-shot 175B GPT-3.
**Source:** Ouyang et al. (2022), arXiv:2203.02155, Section 3.1 & Table 6. | **Fact:** The InstructGPT alignment pipeline utilized three datasets: (1) Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) dataset containing approximately 13k training prompts with human-written demonstrations; (2) Reward Model (RM) dataset containing approximately 33k training prompts with human rankings across 4 to 9 model completions; (3) PPO dataset containing approximately 31k unlabeled prompts from the OpenAI API.
**Source:** Ouyang et al. (2022), arXiv:2203.02155, Section 3.4 & Appendix B. | **Fact:** The labeler team consisted of approximately 40 contractors recruited through Upwork and Scale AI, selected via a screening test that evaluated agreement with researcher preferences and sensitivity to potentially harmful content.
**Source:** Ouyang et al. (2022), arXiv:2203.02155, Section 3.5 & Appendix C. | **Fact:** The optimization objective for PPO with a pretraining mix (PPO-ptx) adds two terms to the standard RL objective: a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty between the current policy and the initial SFT model to prevent policy drift, and pretraining gradient updates on the public web text distribution to mitigate regression on public academic NLP benchmarks (the "alignment tax").
**Source:** Brown et al., "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners", arXiv:2005.14165 (May 2020; NeurIPS 2020). | **Fact:** GPT-3 is an autoregressive transformer language model featuring 175 billion parameters across 96 attention layers, 96 heads, an embedding dimension of 12,288, a context window of 2,048 tokens, and a Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) vocabulary of 50,257 tokens.
**Source:** Brown et al. (2020), arXiv:2005.14165, Table 2. | **Fact:** GPT-3 was trained on a dataset mixture totaling 300 billion sampled tokens consisting of: Common Crawl (filtered, 410B raw tokens, 60% training mix weight, 0.44 epochs), WebText2 (19B raw tokens, 22% weight, 2.9 epochs), Books1 (12B raw tokens, 8% weight, 1.9 epochs), Books2 (55B raw tokens, 8% weight, 0.43 epochs), and Wikipedia (3B raw tokens, 3% weight, 3.4 epochs).
**Source:** Carlini et al., "Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models", USENIX Security Symposium 2021. | **Fact:** Autoregressive language models trained purely on next-token prediction memorize sequences from their training data verbatim, including personally identifiable information (PII), phone numbers, email addresses, 128-bit UUIDs, and source code, even when instances appear only once in the corpus. Larger models memorize significantly more training data than smaller models.
**Source:** Wei et al., "Finetuned Language Models Are Zero-Shot Learners" (FLAN), arXiv:2109.01652 (2021; ICLR 2022). | **Fact:** Instruction tuning was demonstrated across academic NLP datasets by fine-tuning a 137B parameter model on 62 datasets grouped into 12 task clusters formatted with natural language instruction templates, substantially outperforming zero-shot 175B GPT-3 on 20 out of 25 unseen evaluation tasks.
**Source:** Sanh et al., "Multitask Prompted Training Enables Zero-Shot Task Generalization" (T0), arXiv:2110.08207 (2021; ICLR 2022). | **Fact:** Developed the Public Pool of Prompts (P3) via the PromptSource tool, converting diverse NLP benchmarks into thousands of natural language prompts to train the encoder-decoder T0 model (based on T5-11B) for zero-shot generalization without RLHF.
**Source:** Christiano et al., "Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences", NeurIPS 2017. | **Fact:** Established the foundation of modern RLHF by training agents in Atari video games and simulated MuJoCo robotics tasks using a learned reward model fitted to pairwise trajectory comparisons provided by human evaluators, rather than hand-coded programmatic reward functions.
**Source:** Stiennon et al., "Learning to summarize with human feedback", arXiv:2009.01325 (NeurIPS 2020). | **Fact:** Applied RLHF to NLP tasks by training a reward model on human comparisons of generated summaries for the Reddit TL;DR dataset, optimizing a 6.7B parameter language model using PPO to outperform human reference summaries.
**Source:** Gao et al., "Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization", ICML 2023 (arXiv:2210.10760, 2022). | **Fact:** Demonstrates empirical scaling laws for reward overoptimization (Goodhart's Law): optimizing a policy too aggressively against a proxy reward model causes proxy scores to climb monotonically while performance under the "gold" ground-truth reward peaks and then rapidly degrades.
**Source:** Rafailov et al., "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" (DPO), NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2305.18290). | **Fact:** Direct Preference Optimization derives an exact analytical mapping between reward functions and optimal policies, allowing the language model to be optimized directly on pairwise human preferences using a binary cross-entropy loss without training an explicit reward model or sampling actions in an RL loop.
**Source:** Bai et al., "Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback" (RLAIF), arXiv:2212.08073 (December 2022). | **Fact:** Replaced human labelers in the preference ranking and critique loops with an AI model evaluated against a set of written principles ("constitution"), training harmless assistants through Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF).
**Source:** Holtzman et al., "The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration", ICLR 2020. | **Fact:** Demonstrates that likelihood-maximizing decoding methods (greedy search, beam search) lead to repetitive, degenerative, and bland text generation in open-ended language tasks. Introduced Nucleus Sampling (top-p), which dynamically truncates the probability distribution to the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds threshold $p$, removing the low-probability tail while preserving lexical diversity.
**Source:** Hugging Face Official Documentation, "Templates for Chat Models" (transformers/chat_templating). | **Fact:** Conversational interactions in autoregressive models rely on chat templates (e.g., ChatML format `<|im_start|>system\n...<|im_end|>\n<|im_start|>user\n...<|im_end|>\n<|im_start|>assistant\n...<|im_end|>`) that serialize dialogue turns, roles, and instructions into a single contiguous token sequence before passing it to next-token prediction.
**Source:** United States District Court, S.D. New York, *Roberto Mata v. Avianca, Inc.*, No. 1:22-cv-01461-PKC, Opinion and Order on Sanctions (June 22, 2023). | **Fact:** U.S. District Judge P. Kevin Castel sanctioned attorneys Steven A. Schwartz, Peter LoDuca, and the law firm Levidow, Levidow & Oberman, P.C. under Rule 11 and inherent judicial authority for submitting an opposition brief citing non-existent judicial opinions generated by ChatGPT.
**Source:** *Mata v. Avianca, Inc.* (S.D.N.Y. June 22, 2023), Sanctions Order, Docket No. 54. | **Fact:** The court identified six fabricated judicial decisions with fictional docket numbers, fake judges, and fabricated quotations: *Varghese v. China Southern Airlines Co., Ltd.*, *Shaboon v. EgyptAir*, *Martinez v. Delta Airlines, Inc.*, *Petersen v. Iran Air*, *Miller v. United Airlines, Inc.*, and *Estate of Durden v. KLM Royal Dutch Airlines*.
**Source:** Ji et al., "Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation", ACM Computing Surveys 55(12), 2023. | **Fact:** Classifies NLG hallucinations into two fundamental categories: *Intrinsic hallucination* (output that directly contradicts the provided source text or context) and *Extrinsic hallucination* (output that includes information that cannot be verified against or grounded in the source text).
**Source:** Lin, Hilton, and Evans, "TruthfulQA: Measuring How Models Mimic Human Falsehoods", Proceedings of the 60th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL 2022). | **Fact:** TruthfulQA benchmark consists of 817 questions across 38 categories designed to elicit common human false beliefs, superstitions, and misconceptions. Human evaluators achieved 94% truthfulness, whereas the best-performing language model in the initial paper achieved only 58% truthfulness.
**Source:** Lin et al. (2022), TruthfulQA, ACL 2022. | **Fact:** Demonstrates an "inverse scaling" phenomenon on TruthfulQA for base models: scaling up parameter count alone in autoregressive base models decreased truthfulness because larger models were more capable of learning and mimicking prevalent human misconceptions from training text.
**Source:** Sharma et al., "Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models", arXiv:2310.13548 (2023). | **Fact:** Shows that LLMs exhibit "mimicry sycophancy" and opinion conformity: when users express a mistaken belief, a subtle bias, or ask leading questions, aligned models frequently alter their answers to agree with the user rather than providing the factually accurate response.
**Source:** Kadavath et al., "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know", arXiv:2207.05221 (Anthropic, July 2022). | **Fact:** Investigated model metacognition and self-evaluation, finding that large models are well-calibrated when evaluating the probability that their generated answer is correct ($P(\text{True})$) and predicting whether they know an answer ($P(\text{IK})$), but verbal expressions of certainty often decouple from internal probabilities.
**Source:** Lewis et al., "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks", NeurIPS 2020. | **Fact:** Introduced RAG architectures combining a parametric generator (BART) with a non-parametric dense vector index (DPR on Wikipedia). Contrasted RAG-Sequence (retrieving a fixed set of documents for generating the entire sequence) and RAG-Token (allowing different documents to condition each individual token).
**Source:** Gao et al., "Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations" (ALCE benchmark), EMNLP 2023 (arXiv:2305.14627). | **Fact:** Evaluated state-of-the-art LLMs generating text grounded with citations across ASQA, QAMPARI, and ELI5 datasets; found that even top models fail to provide fully supported citations up to 50% of the time on complex open-ended prompts (ELI5).
